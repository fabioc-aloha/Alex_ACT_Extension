# Put Alex to work: the CSAR loop

Most people jump straight to **Act**. They tell Alex what to do and start collecting output. That's **vibe coding**: turn after turn with no shared understanding, no check that the right thing is being built, no reflection on whether it works.

The cure is a four-beat conversational loop that cycles many times before the work is done:

- **C — Clarify.** Surface what's unclear *before* doing anything. Alex should be asking you questions. You should be asking Alex back. *"Name the assumptions you're making. I'll correct any that are wrong."*
- **S — Summarize.** Restate the problem before solving it. The frame audit. Alex hands back its understanding in one sentence; you read it back in your own words. Mismatch shows up here while it's cheap.
- **A — Act.** Now do the work, with the frame visible. When the work pulls away from the frame, name it: *"Stop. That decision wasn't in our plan."*
- **R — Reflect.** Check what happened. *"Where is this weakest? What would a hostile reviewer attack first?"* Without Reflect, you can't tell good work from confident-looking work.

Reflect often sends you back to Clarify. That's the loop working, not failing.

You've dropped the loop when Alex never asks questions, when you can't say in one sentence what's being built, or when the conversation ends with *"thanks, looks good"* and nothing got pressure-tested. When you notice it, restart from Clarify.
